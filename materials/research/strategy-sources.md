## TradingView public scripts and reusable ideas

TradingView's scripts directory, Tools profile, and India Tools script listing were requested, but the live fetch service returned “restricted URL” for all three. I therefore could not rank scripts by use or verify individual script pages and their displayed license labels. **Popularity and per-script license are unverified.** TradingView distinguishes open, protected, and invite-only scripts; open-source scripts default to MPL-2.0 only when no different license is specified. Protected and invite-only scripts hide source, and invite-only also restricts use. A script page's own license and visibility must be checked before any reuse. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [TradingView scripts](https://www.tradingview.com/scripts/), [Tools profile](https://www.tradingview.com/u/Tools/), [India Tools scripts](https://in.tradingview.com/scripts/tools/), [script types](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000482573-what-are-the-different-types-of-published-scripts/), [Pine publishing docs](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/v5/writing/publishing/), [TradingView Terms](https://www.tradingview.com/policies/).)

The following are familiar, independently specified strategy/indicator ideas to consider—not a verified “most-used” ranking from those inaccessible lists. For every idea, per-script license is **unverified** until a specific script page is reviewed. We may implement the mathematical idea from an independent public or standard definition; we must not copy Pine code. TradingView's rules also say source reuse obligations depend on the script's chosen license and its rules for reuse. TradingView's terms restrict using its content to build a competing service, so counsel or TradingView should resolve that issue before basing a competing product on material obtained from TradingView. The recommendations below rely on independently stated rules and existing project materials, not a scrape or transcription of script listings. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [script publishing rules](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000590599-script-publishing-rules/), [Terms](https://www.tradingview.com/policies/).)

| Idea | Plain definition | License shown on specific TradingView script page | Reimplement from independent public definition? |
|---|---|---|---|
| VWAP | Volume-weighted average price is cumulative price times volume divided by cumulative volume for the session. Traders use it as an intraday reference; daily OHLC bars cannot reproduce true intraday VWAP faithfully. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from standard formula; daily-only implementation must be labeled an approximation. |
| Anchored VWAP | Same volume-weighted calculation, reset at a selected event/date rather than the session open. It measures average traded price since that anchor. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from independently published formula; do not copy code. |
| Supertrend | A volatility band based on average true range flips direction when price crosses the trailing band. Exact ATR length, multiplier, and band carry-forward variants differ by script. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, only after fixing an independent definition and parameters. |
| MACD | Difference between a fast and slow exponential moving average, commonly paired with a signal EMA and histogram. It describes trend and momentum; it is lagging. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from the standard formula. |
| Bollinger Bands / squeeze | Bands commonly place a chosen number of standard deviations around a moving average; a squeeze marks relatively low band width. A squeeze alone does not determine breakout direction. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from a published formula; define standard-deviation and population/sample convention. |
| RSI divergence | RSI compares average gains and losses over a lookback; divergence flags disagreement between price pivots and RSI pivots. Pivot confirmation arrives late and is sensitive to pivot settings. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, with explicit RSI smoothing and pivot confirmation rules. |
| Opening-range breakout | Define a range from the first fixed interval after the open, then signal on a later break above or below it. It requires intraday bars and a session calendar. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from an independent written definition; outside a daily-bar engine. |
| Moving-average ribbon | Several moving averages with distinct lengths visualize trend alignment and compression. A ribbon is a display unless entry and exit conditions are specified. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from standard averages; define all lengths and triggers. |
| Donchian channel breakout | Upper/lower bands are highest high and lowest low over a prior lookback. A close above the prior upper band can define a long breakout; the exit window is a separate rule. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes; see the dated Turtle rule source below. |
| SMA crossover | Compare two simple moving averages and signal when the shorter average crosses the longer. It is a transparent trend-following baseline with delayed signals. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from the arithmetic definition. |
| EMA crossover | Compare faster and slower exponentially weighted price averages. The weighting makes recent observations count more than older observations. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from an independently specified EMA formula. |
| RSI 70/30 | RSI is bounded from 0 to 100; conventional overbought/oversold reference levels are 70 and 30. These levels describe momentum extremes, not guaranteed reversal points. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, from standard RSI definitions; avoid presenting thresholds as predictive facts. |
| ATR trailing stop | Average true range summarizes price range including gaps; a stop trails price by a chosen ATR multiple. A close-based daily test cannot promise an intraday stop fill. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, with exact ATR, update, and fill rules. |
| Fibonacci retracement levels | Ratios of a selected price swing are used to draw possible pullback reference levels. Anchor selection is subjective unless the swing-detection rule is made mechanical. | Unverified; script-specific. | Yes, as arithmetic ratios; strategy trigger needs an independently specified swing rule. |

Ideas above are general descriptions, not claims that each appeared in the requested feeds. No author-specific variant is represented as a standard definition. (Retrieved 2026-11-17.)

## Yahoo Finance articles

Yahoo's pages for the first two articles returned HTTP 429 to direct fetch; their search-indexed article text supplied the summaries below. The third article was also accessible only in indexed text, which exposed substantial content but not the full list of 12 tips. Summaries and lists are consequently limited to what was visible. Treat promotional anecdotes, personal experience, and uncited outcome statements as claims by the authors, not evidence of expected results. No strategy performance claim is repeated here. (Retrieved 2026-11-17.)

### “6 Day Trading Strategies, And How To Use Them” — Ross Cameron / Warrior Trading, 2017

Summary: The author describes six short-term setups and emphasizes repeated routines, premarket scanning, volume, float, chart patterns, quick entries, and stops. These are the six named: **Gap and Go, momentum trading, bull flags, breakout trading, pullbacks, and reversal trading.** Examples include buying a break of an opening candle after a large premarket gap, and buying a pullback after a strong run. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [Yahoo Finance article](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/6-day-trading-strategies-them-184013259.html).)

Caution: examples and first-person “success” language are not controlled evidence. The article's described intraday setups require intraday data, realistic spreads/slippage, halt and gap handling, and a defined universe; daily bars cannot test the stated entries. The author reports a per-share gain/loss ratio for reversal trades without a full sample, dates, costs, or distribution; do not treat it as an expected return. (Retrieved 2026-11-17.)

### “A Guide to Stock Trading Strategies” — Eric Reed / SmartAsset, 2020

Summary: The guide contrasts passive investing with active trading, then discusses **pair trading, day trading, trading the news, fundamental-analysis trading, technical-analysis trading, arbitrage, and a speculation/investment split**. It describes day trading as same-day holding, pair trading as long one related asset and short another, and arbitrage as attempting to exploit simultaneous price differences. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [Yahoo Finance article](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/guide-stock-trading-strategies-153119835.html).)

Caution: the article describes fees, leverage, and relative risk in broad terms. Its arbitrage example omits venue fees, transfer/settlement constraints, latency, borrow, and execution risk; an apparent price gap is not a risk-free realized trade. Its 10–40% speculative allocation range is presented without a source or suitability analysis. Treat these as editorial explanations, not universal prescriptions. (Retrieved 2026-11-17.)

### “12 Best Day Trading Tips for Beginners” — Mashaid Ahmed / Insider Monkey, 2024

Summary: The visible article promotes education, starting small, calculating risk/reward, emotional discipline, choosing a niche, and using stop losses. It also cites trader educators and market tools such as anchored VWAP, volume, VIX, and average daily range. The complete 12-tip list was not available in the fetched text, so the remaining tip names are **unverified**. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [Yahoo Finance article](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/12-best-day-trading-tips-135448213.html).)

Caution: the article uses unsourced quotes and trader earnings/profit assertions, an unsourced “1:2” risk/reward heuristic, and generalized statements about leverage and stop-loss protection. Stops do not guarantee a fill at the stop price; gaps and fast markets can worsen execution. Starting with small capital limits dollar exposure but does not establish positive expectancy. (Retrieved 2026-11-17.)

## Robinhood product and lesson mapping

**Robinhood Strategies is a managed-portfolio service**, provided by Robinhood Asset Management, an SEC-registered investment adviser. The firm describes an actively managed portfolio tailored to investor profile, goals, horizon, and risk tolerance; its investment team chooses and adjusts holdings. Minimum is **$50**; below **$500** accounts are invested in ETFs, while accounts of $500 or more may include ETFs and individual stocks. Management fee is **0.25% per year**, accrued daily and billed monthly; eligible Gold members pay the fee only on the first $100,000 per managed account (Gold subscription is $5 monthly or $50 annually according to the FAQ). Other account/underlying-fund fees may apply. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [Strategies FAQ](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/strategies-faq/), [product page](https://robinhood.com/us/en/strategies/), [investment approach](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/strategies-investment-approach/).)

The team decides portfolio trades and may adjust the allocation; the customer supplies goals/profile and can toggle some individual-stock preferences. The product page says customers cannot contact the investment team directly. The firm's process spans asset-universe selection, asset allocation, quantitative factor analysis, qualitative review, and daily monitoring. This is a managed-account product, not a self-directed strategy signal. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: same official pages above.)

Robinhood's general investing tools show holdings and customizable sort fields such as last price, percentage change, equity, and returns; Options Insights shows selected-period net options performance and open/closed-position history. The Strategies dashboard charts overall return net of management fees, portfolio allocation, and a return breakdown including appreciation/depreciation, income, and fees. The overall return is described as a simple return measure that does not account for compounding or cash flows; benchmark comparisons and future projections need their stated caveats. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [investing tools](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/investing-tools/), [Strategies charts](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/strategies-charts/).)

| Product feature | Lesson connection |
|---|---|
| Managed service, advisor identity, who decides trades | Product passport: identify the legal product, provider, decision-maker, and customer control. |
| 0.25% fee, Gold subscription, ETF expense ratios, other charges | Risk and fees: calculate recurring costs and distinguish management fee from total cost. |
| $50 minimum; $500 threshold for individual stocks | Product passport and access constraints: minimums alter the available portfolio. |

## Backtest engine additions: recommended next rules

Keep all signals on completed daily bars and fill at the next session's open, consistent with the current engine contract in `materials/research/strategies.md`. Use long/flat rules; no leverage or shorting. Source texts describe the rule families; the exact close-confirmed daily implementations below are explicit lab conventions where they differ from intraday source rules. Supports Part 2 (quantitative strategies), Part 5 (research discipline/data provenance), Lesson 23 (signals, math, execution simulation), Lesson 30 (backtests with baselines and costs), and Lesson 32 (controls and safe failure) in the current curriculum. (Retrieved 2026-11-17: [curriculum](../LESSON_PLAN_V2.md), [strategy catalog](strategies.md).)

| 1 | **Donchian 20/10 long breakout.** At close t, enter long next open if close[t] exceeds the highest high of sessions t−20 through t−1. While long, exit next open if close[t] falls below the lowest low of t−10 through t−1. Otherwise hold; one position, no short. | Turtle System 1's published channel-breakout family uses a 20-day breakout and 10-day exit. Long/flat, one position. | Connors-style rule summarized by StockCharts; RSI smoothing and next-open fill must be explicit. | 6 | **Bollinger band re-entry, long/flat.** Use SMA20 and population standard deviation of the prior 20 closes including t; lower band = SMA20−2σ. Enter next open when close[t−1] was below its lower band and close[t] closes back above the lower band. Exit next open when close[t] reaches or exceeds SMA20[t]. | Explicit test convention based on Bollinger's published bands; confirm exact standard-deviation convention before release.
